Link building in the Philippines is sold as a volume product more often than it is practised as a trust product. The pitch is a number: 50 links, 100 links, 500 links, DA screenshots, and a spreadsheet of domains you have never heard of. The work that actually supports a page is slower and more boring. Someone has to earn or place a mention on a site that a human would read, on a topic adjacent to yours, pointing at a URL that already deserves to rank.

Google does not owe you a ranking because a vendor invoiced a quota. Links are one way the web says a page is a reference, not a decoration. If the linking sites exist to sell links, they are not references. They are inventory. Inventory can look busy in a report and still do nothing, or worse, attach your domain to a neighbourhood you will later have to explain.

## What A Link Is For

A useful link does three jobs. It can send a qualified human. It can help discovery when a crawler finds you from a page it already trusts. It can support the entity and topic of the destination URL. Most package links do none of those. They sit in a footer, a blog comment, a “resources” page of 200 unrelated URLs, or an article spun for the sole purpose of hosting anchors.

Relevance beats metrics. A regional supplier, an industry association, a university partner page, a local news story about a real event, or a genuine guest article on a trade site will usually outweigh a high-DA blog that sells posts to anyone with a credit card. Domain-level scores are easy to fake with networks. Topical overlap is harder to fake.

The destination URL matters as much as the source. Links to the homepage because the vendor’s form only has one field waste the mention. Links to a thin blog post because that was easy to “optimise” waste it in a different way. Point mentions at the commercial page that should rank, once that page is actually good. Link building does not repair a page that cannot explain the offer.

Anchors should read like language. Exact-match commercial phrases in bulk, especially from junk sites, are a pattern, not a strategy. Brand name, URL, and natural phrases in a sentence are how real citations look. If the monthly report is 40 identical “best [service] Philippines” anchors, you are looking at a template, not at editorial placement.

## Why The Local Market Is Full Of Packages

There is real demand. Philippine SMEs are told they need “off-page SEO”. Vendors compete on price. Price competition on a thing that is hard to inspect produces factories: PBNs, expired domains, sitewide widget links, and guest-post networks that recycle the same 300 blogs.

Those factories are easy to operate from anywhere. Geography does not make a link local. A mention on a real Manila publication about a real opening is local. A blog with a .ph domain, no byline, and ten outbound posts a day about casinos, loans, and roofing is not local. It is a skin.

Buyers cannot easily verify quality, so they verify quantity. “We got 80 links” feels like delivery. “We got four mentions, here are the URLs, here is why they exist” feels small. The second is often the only work worth keeping. The first is how [cheap backlink packages fail](/blog/why-cheap-backlink-packages-fail/): they optimise for the spreadsheet, not for the neighbourhood your domain will live in.

Agencies that refuse packages look expensive next to a 5,000 PHP “boost”. They are expensive in the way that not buying a problem is expensive. Disavows, brand-serps full of spam, and months of recovery are the deferred invoice.

## What Honest Link Work Looks Like In Practice

Start with the pages that already have a job. Service, category, location, and comparison URLs that are crawlable, unique, and able to convert. If those pages are thin, spend the month on them. A link to an empty URL is a vote for nothing.

Then look for mentions you should already have. Unlinked brand mentions, supplier directories you actually belong to, chamber or association listings, alumni pages, event pages, software marketplaces, and partner logos that never got an href. This is unglamorous and often higher value than a guest post on a stranger’s blog.

Digital PR is the grown-up version: a story, data, a comment from someone who did the work, or a resource other sites would cite without being paid. That needs something worth talking about. A “10 tips” article is not a story. Original pricing research, a method, a public dataset, or a genuine project with permission to discuss it can be.

Guest contributions still work when the host has readers in your industry and the piece would be publishable without the link. They fail when the host’s only traffic is other SEOs. Ask for referral data, or look at whether the site ranks for anything that is not “write for us”.

Local citations are a separate product. Consistent NAP on real directories can support Maps. They are not a substitute for editorial links, and stuffing 200 citations overnight is another package pattern. Pick the directories a customer might use. Ignore the rest.

## What To Refuse Even When The Price Looks Friendly

Refuse any offer that prices links by the hundred with no URL preview. You should see placements before they go live, or at least a tight list of sites with a right of refusal. After-the-fact spreadsheets are how bad neighbourhoods get attached to your domain while you are at lunch.

Refuse guaranteed rankings bundled with links. Nobody who understands search can promise position one because they bought you 50 mentions. Forecasts based on current impressions and content gaps are different. Guarantees are a sales device.

Refuse sitewide footer and sidebar links on unrelated domains, comment blasts, and “homepage link” upsells on expired sites. Refuse networks you cannot visit as a user. If the page has no author, no about information, no organic traffic beyond other link pages, and outbound anchors in a grid, it exists to sell links.

Refuse to let the vendor own the relationships in a way you cannot see. You should receive the live URLs in an account you control. If the only proof is a PDF, you cannot audit, and you cannot keep the assets when you leave.

Refuse to mix a new link campaign with an unknown old one. If a previous vendor already attached spam, adding more of the same is not a strategy. Crawl the backlink profile first. Clean, then earn.

## How To Brief And Measure Without Counting The Wrong Thing

Name the commercial URLs. Name the topics they should be associated with. Name any relationships you already have: suppliers, clients who will not mind a mention, associations, campuses, events, publications that have run your work before. That list is the beginning of a programme. A vendor who ignores it in favour of their inventory is not building your graph. They are filling theirs.

Ask how they will prospect. Manual outreach, digital PR, partner enablement, and resource pages are real methods. “Our network” is not a method unless you can inspect the network.

Ask what they will not touch. A serious practitioner will have a no list. If they will place anything you pay for, you are the quality control, and you will lose.

Measure referral visits, branded search, and whether the destination URLs gain impressions for the topics they were meant to support. Do not measure success as raw referring-domain count in the first 30 days. New links can take time to be crawled, and many never should have been. Search Console links reports and a crawler export are more honest than a vendor’s DA chart.

Pace matters. A new domain that acquires 200 links in a week from sites with no topical overlap does not look like a popular company. It looks like a campaign. Real businesses accrue mentions as they do things in public. Your link graph should roughly resemble your real-world activity, even if you are helping that activity along.

## How Link Building Fits The Rest Of SEO

Links do not fix robots.txt mistakes, duplicate location pages, or a product template with no unique copy. They can support a page that is already the better result. Sequence is the adult conversation. Technical and on-page first, then mentions that point at the result you want.

Content that attracts links has to be linkable. That usually means it is usable without you: a calculator, a specification, a definition that is actually correct, original figures, or a demonstration. Blog posts that exist to host keywords are rarely cited. If the content plan cannot name why another site would mention the piece, it is not a link asset. It is a page for searchers, which is still useful, just not a magnet.

Brand SERP hygiene sits next to this. If the first page for your company name is spam profiles and scraper directories, some of that is leftover package work. Cleaning those entities is part of reputation and part of search. Buying more of them is not.

For local service businesses, Maps, reviews, and a complete profile will often move enquiries faster than a guest-post campaign. Do not skip local trust because a vendor prefers off-page retainers. For national and B2B, the opposite mistake is also common: citations and social bookmarks instead of industry mentions.

## A Practical Way To Buy This In The Philippines

Do not start with a quota. Start with a crawl of what already points at you, a list of commercial URLs, and a short list of real-world relationships. Then buy a month of prospecting and placement against that list, with URLs in writing.

If a vendor cannot work that way, they are a package shop. Package shops can still be honest about being cheap and limited. They become a problem when they promise authority. Authority is not something you purchase in blocks.

If you already bought a package, export the URLs, look at them in a browser, and decide what is harmless noise versus what needs a removal request or a disavow conversation. Do not disavow casually. Do not ignore a profile that is obviously industrial spam either. Get a specialist to look before you submit files in Search Console.

If you have never done outreach, expect it to feel slow. People ignore email. Editors say no. Partners take weeks to update a logo page. That friction is a quality signal. Frictionless links are the ones that were pre-sold.
