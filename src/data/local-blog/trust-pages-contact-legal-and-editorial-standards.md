Trust pages are the unglamorous URLs that tell a stranger the company is real, reachable and bound by rules. Contact, privacy, terms and editorial standards will not win a vanity keyword on their own. They decide whether the rest of the site looks like an operator or a landing page that could vanish on Monday.

Google’s quality rater guidelines put trust at the centre of E-E-A-T. An untrustworthy site rates poorly even if the copy sounds expert. Missing contact information, no owner, no policy for claims, and legal pages copied from another domain are how that judgement gets made.

We keep [how to reach us](/contact/), the [privacy policy](/privacy-policy/) and our [editorial standards](/editorial-standards/) on the same public record as the about copy and named authors. They should agree with each other.

## What Counts As A Trust Page

Contact: how a human gets hold of a human. Phone, form, email, address, hours if they are real.

Legal: privacy, terms, and any sector notice you are actually required to give. Cookie copy that matches what the site does.

Identity: about, team, author profiles, company details that match filings and profiles elsewhere.

Standards: how you write, what you will not claim, how you correct mistakes, how you report results.

Some firms add complaints, accessibility, or a modern slavery statement because the law or the client requires it. Do not add those as decoration. An empty accessibility statement is worse than linking to a working contact route and fixing the actual barriers.

## Contact Has To Work

The contact URL is often the first page a sceptical buyer or a rater will try. If the form errors, the phone is dead, or the address is a coworking lobby you never enter, the rest of the pitch is discounted.

Put the same name, city and channels on the contact URL, the footer, the about copy and any LocalBusiness markup. We operate from Quezon City. That fact should not change depending on which template you loaded.

A form needs a destination that someone reads. Auto-replies that never lead to a person are not contact. Chat widgets that cannot hand off are not contact. Tracking numbers that expire after a campaign are not contact.

If you use a CRM, test the path after every site deploy. Forms break quietly. The visitor does not file a bug report. They leave.

Do not hide the contact URL behind a login or a bot gauntlet that blocks ordinary browsers. If you need spam control, use it in a way that still lets a customer through.

## Legal Copy Should Describe This Site

Privacy policies fail when they are pasted from a generator and still mention tools you do not use, countries you do not operate in, or a company name from the last client the designer had.

Write what you collect, why, where it is stored, and how someone asks for deletion. If the form sends to email, say so. If you use analytics, name the class of tool. If you do not sell personal data, say that only if it is true.

Terms of service should describe the actual engagement: what a discussion is, what a proposal is, what you do not guarantee. We do not sell guaranteed rankings or guaranteed AI inclusion. That belongs in commercial copy and should not be contradicted by a terms file that promises results.

Update the legal pages when the stack changes. A privacy file that still describes a chat tool you removed is a small lie that sits next to your trust claims.

Link privacy and terms from the footer and from the form. People should not have to guess that they exist.

## Editorial Standards Are The Missing Trust Page For Agencies

Most service sites have contact and a privacy file. Almost none say how they handle proof. That gap matters in SEO because the industry is famous for invented graphs.

Our standards page states who writes and who checks, what counts as evidence, how we talk about results, and what we will not publish. Public pages and client recommendations are written by named people. Strategy sits with Ben Lowe. Campaign delivery is run by the in-house team in Quezon City, including Jani A. Cañonero and Jewe O. Manalo. Senior review happens before a recommendation is treated as ready to implement.

Evidence is Search Console, ranking software with engine and location stated, crawls, and enquiry events the business already tracks. Traffic on its own is not a win. We do not invent clients, quotes, rankings, traffic, leads, revenue or AI citations. We do not name client websites by default. We do not publish public rate cards. We do not mint thin city pages for offices we do not have.

If we get a fact wrong on this site, we fix the page. Hiding the correction is a trust failure.

You can copy that structure for a trade business without copying our services. Who does the work. What proof looks like. What you will not say in public. How a customer complains. How you change a published error.

## Consistency Across The Record

Trust pages only work as a set. The contact city must match about. The people named in standards must exist on the team grid. The privacy email must reach someone. The author profiles must use the same names as the bylines.

Search systems reconcile entities. So do journalists, procurement teams and anyone who has been burnt by a lead-gen site. One mismatch is a mistake. Three mismatches look like a front.

Keep the company LinkedIn, Google Business Profile and footer in the same family. If the legal name differs from the trading name, say both once, clearly.

Schema must follow the visible pages. Do not mark up a full address you are unwilling to show. Do not mark up a founder who is not named. Do not attach aggregate ratings that are not on the page.

## What Raters And Buyers Do With These URLs

A rater looking at reputation and website information will look for who is responsible, whether the site provides adequate information about the business, and whether customer-service information exists. They are not scoring your privacy policy for literature. They are checking that it exists, that it seems to belong to this business, and that the site is not a scam pattern.

A buyer does something similar with less theory. They want a phone or a form, a name, and a sense that if the work goes wrong there is a person on the other end. Legal pages are a signal that the firm expects to still be here after the invoice.

For YMYL-adjacent services the bar is higher. Even for ordinary trades, a site with no contact and no owner is a site you do not invite into your house.

## Build Order

Ship a working contact route before you ship another service URL.
Add privacy and terms that describe this stack.
Publish identity: about, people, location.
Publish standards if you make public claims about results or methods.
Align footer, schema and off-site profiles.
Test the form on desktop and mobile after deploy.
Review the set when staff, office or tools change.

Do not wait for a redesign. A plain HTML contact URL that works beats a branded page that does not submit.

## Common Failures We See On Client Sites

The only phone number is in an image.
The form posts to a 404.
Privacy still names a UK company after the site moved to a Philippine operator.
Terms forbid screenshots while the marketing team publishes other people’s Search Console.
The editorial or claims policy does not exist, so sales and delivery contradict each other.
Cookie banners list vendors the tag manager does not contain.
The contact URL is noindexed by accident.

Each of those is cheaper to fix than another month of content that nobody believes.

## How This Supports The Rest Of SEO

Technical work, content and digital PR all land better when the domain looks like a firm. Links to a ghost site transfer less. Reviews on a business that cannot be contacted help less. Article bylines on a company with no address help less.

Trust pages will not replace a useful service URL. They stop useful service URLs from sitting on a property that looks disposable.

They also constrain sales. Once you have published what you will not claim, it is harder for a pitch deck to promise the opposite. That is the point. Trust is a restriction you accept in public.

## Keep Them Alive

Assign an owner. Contact breaks. Legal copy drifts. Staff leave. If nobody owns the trust set, it rots.

When you correct a public fact, update the page that stated it. When you change analytics, update privacy. When you change who may byline, update standards and the author grid together.

A trust page is not a launch artefact. It is operations, published.

If your contact route, legal copy and claims policy currently disagree, fix the disagreement before you ask search to treat the domain as a reliable source. Identity first, then proof, then more pages.
