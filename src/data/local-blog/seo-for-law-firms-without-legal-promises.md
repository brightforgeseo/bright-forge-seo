People hiring a lawyer do not search for a slogan. They search the problem: an injury, a family dispute, a property transaction, an employment issue. The firm that ranks is the firm that has a clear URL for that problem, a person who actually does the work, and a way to enquire that a stressed visitor can finish. None of that requires a promised court result. We refuse to write legal SEO that sells outcomes we cannot stand behind.

Legal pages sit in Google's Your Money or Your Life treatment. Thin copy, invented awards, and guaranteed rankings are not a grey area. They are a reason the page gets ignored, and a reason a regulator or a counterpart firm can make your marketing a problem. The SEO job is visibility and intake for the practice areas you already handle. Legal accuracy stays with the firm. Structure, measurement and claim restraint stay with us.

## What Legal Searchers Are Trying To Do

A search for a practice area is a hiring query, not a request for a lecture. The visitor wants to know whether this firm handles that matter, in that place, and what happens after they get in touch. They will look for the solicitor or attorney by name. They will look for reviews. They will leave if the only next step is a chatbot that cannot book a consultation.

Informational queries still exist. People ask how a process works, what a filing involves, or what to bring to a first meeting. Those URLs can support the practice-area URL if they are accurate and internally linked. They cannot replace it. A library of generic explainers that never names the practice, the people or the intake path is how law firm blogs stay busy and the commercial URLs stay invisible.

We will not use scare copy that implies a result. "You may be entitled" without a basis, countdown timers on consultations, and banners that read like a verdict are marketing the firm cannot defend. Clear process, eligibility boundaries, and a calm enquiry path convert better with the clients you actually want, and they do not create a claim file for someone else.

## Practice Areas Own The Query

Most firms bury five practice areas on one Services list, then wonder why none of them rank. Injury, family, employment and property are different SERPs. Each one needs its own URL, its own H1, its own proof, and its own intake. A combined page cannot win every hiring query at once.

The practice-area URL should say what the firm does, who does it, where it is done, and how to start. Credentials belong on the page if the firm can produce them. Reviews belong on the page if they are real. Related guides can support it. What should not live there is a promise that a matter will succeed, a recovery figure with no source, or a ranking guarantee.

Lawyer profiles are part of the same system. People look up the individual they might instruct. Stock biographies that could sit on any firm site do not help, and copied biographies are a trust failure. We structure profile URLs so the person, the practice areas and the office match. We will not invent bar admissions, languages, or case results to fill a template.

Location URLs only cover offices or markets the firm actually serves. A map of cities the firm cannot represent is a doorway pattern, and it is a professional-conduct risk. If you take instructions in one city and occasionally travel, say that. Do not publish ten near-duplicate city URLs with the same paragraph.

## Claims We Will Not Write

We do not write medical or legal outcome guarantees. We do not invent dollar recoveries, settlement figures, or win rates. If the firm has a published, sourced result it is allowed to use in marketing, the firm supplies it and remains responsible for it. If the figure cannot be sourced, it does not go on the page.

We also will not invent rankings, traffic, reviews, awards, or offices. "Award-winning" with no award is a lie. "Leading firm" with no basis is puffery we would rather cut. Star ratings in schema that do not match visible reviews are a structured-data trap. LegalService markup is useful when the visible page already describes the service. It is not a shortcut around thin content.

Guaranteed first-page rankings in thirty days are a sales pitch, not a method. Legal markets are local and competitive. We work from Search Console, the current practice-area URLs, and the SERP. Anyone promising a position is asking you to buy a problem.

This restraint is not a disclaimer paragraph bolted onto aggressive copy. It is the copy. The page should still be useful: process, documents, likely timelines in ranges the firm will stand behind, and who to contact. Usefulness does not require a verdict.

## How We Measure Law Firm Seo

We track rankings and clicks, then whether contact forms, calls and booked consultations are captured. A traffic lift with no intake tracking is incomplete. Where tracking is missing, we say so. We will not fill the gap with estimated case values.

The public proof we can show for legal work is Search Console, not recoveries. A personal-injury law firm we already published reached 2,065 organic clicks in a 90-day window, a 106.5 percent lift, with stronger average position and a 0.60 percent click-through rate. Clicks doubled from a modest rise in exposure. We do not name the firm. We do not translate those clicks into settlements. Clicks are clicks. Consultations are a separate measurement that only exists if the firm records them.

Brand queries and non-brand practice queries should be split in the report. A television campaign that lifts the firm name is not practice-area SEO. A rise in informational clicks that never reach intake is not a hiring win. The useful scoreboard is the practice-area URLs, the queries they win, and the consultations those URLs produce.

## Trust Signals That Belong On The Hiring Url

Reviews, professional listings, and clear contact details belong on the practice-area URL, not only on About. A visitor who has just searched an injury query should not have to hunt for a phone number. NAP consistency across the site, Google Business Profile, and directories still matters for local legal search. Inconsistent addresses look like two firms.

Author names on legal articles should be people who can stand behind the words. Ghost-written explainers with a partner's name and no review are a YMYL risk. We would rather publish fewer guides that a named lawyer has checked than a calendar of unreviewed posts.

Technical basics still apply. Mobile forms that fail, slow templates, mixed http links, and crawl errors on lawyer profiles will cap any content work. Schema should reflect the visible organisation, office and service. FAQ markup only where the questions are on the page.

## Editorial Rules, Not Theatre

The public rule for how we write and report sits on our [editorial standards](/editorial-standards/). We do not invent clients, traffic, rankings or revenue. We do not name client websites by default. Proof is the method, the data source and the change in the account. Legal SEO is where those rules are least optional, because the visitor is making a high-stakes hiring decision and the firm is regulated.

Senior review happens before a recommendation is treated as ready to implement. The firm fact-checks legal accuracy. We fact-check that the page structure, metadata, internal links and intake path match the offer. If the firm will not review copy, we are not the right team. If the firm wants us to invent results to compete with a noisier competitor, we are not the right team.

## What The Work Covers

[Legal SEO](/legal-seo-services/) is practice-area URLs first, lawyer pages that do a job, location coverage only where the office is real, trust on the hiring URL, intake that can be measured, and technical and schema basics that match the visible page. It is not a content mill for every legal topic under the sun.

It suits firms that already know which matters they want more of, will fact-check the copy, and can handle extra consultations. It does not suit a firm that wants every city in the country on a map, or a partner who wants a recovery figure on the hero because a competitor used one.

Internal links should follow the hiring journey. A process guide points to the practice-area URL. The practice-area URL points to the people and the intake. The people point back to the matters they handle. That is enough. We do not spray every article with every practice to "build authority".
