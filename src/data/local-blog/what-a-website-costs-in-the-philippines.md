Quotes for “a website” in the Philippines can be 15,000 PHP or 400,000 PHP and still sound like the same brief on the phone. They are not the same product. One is often a theme, five pages, and a handover PDF. The other is templates, content models, QA, redirects, and a site that can take an enquiry on a mid-range phone. Comparing those numbers without naming the scope is how businesses buy a brochure and then wonder why ads and SEO feel expensive.

Treat the bands below as market ranges, not as a public rate card. Freelancers and agencies overlap. A senior freelancer can outrun a junior agency. A cheap agency can still be a freelancer with a logo. The decision is what has to exist on launch day, who owns the CMS, and what happens when something breaks.

## What You Are Actually Buying

A website is not a design file. It is hosting, a CMS or a static build, templates, copy, forms, analytics, and the ability to change a page without calling a stranger. Price follows the number of unique templates, the complexity of the catalogue or booking logic, the quality of the content, and how much testing anyone does before DNS cuts over.

Philippine buyers often specify page count because it feels concrete. Page count is a weak driver. Ten pages that share one template are cheap. Ten pages that each need a unique layout, bilingual copy, and a calculator are not. Ask for templates, not for a page tally: home, service, location, article, category, product, contact. Extra URLs that reuse a template should not multiply the quote linearly.

Content is the other silent driver. If you cannot supply services, proof, images, and legal pages, someone writes them or the site ships with lorem and stock handshakes. Writing is work. Photography is work. “We will use what is on Facebook” is how the new site looks like the old one with new colours.

SEO is not a free extra and it is not a plugin tickbox. Redirects, titles, heading logic, crawlable HTML, and a conversion path are part of a commercial site. If those are missing, you will pay again through [web development](/development/) or through wasted media. A pretty theme that ships noindex leftovers from staging is not a bargain.

## Freelancer Market Bands

Junior freelancers in the market often quote 8,000 to 25,000 PHP for a basic brochure: a theme, a handful of pages, a contact form, and little testing. That can be rational for a personal brand, a pop-up offer, or a holding page. It is a poor foundation for a business that needs search traffic, because the moment you need a second template, a developer who has moved on, or a plugin conflict, you start again.

Mid-level freelancers commonly sit around 15,000 to 60,000 PHP for a small business site on WordPress or a similar CMS. You might get a cleaner theme, basic on-page fields, and a WhatsApp button that works. You might not get a staging environment, a redirect map, accessibility checks, or anyone else who can pick up the project if they disappear.

Senior freelancers and specialists can quote 25,000 to 150,000 PHP depending on ecommerce, custom post types, and whether they are also doing information architecture. At the top of that band you are paying for judgement, not just hours. You are still paying one person. Illness, overseas contracts, and “I am full until next quarter” are part of the model.

Freelance hourly rates in the market often run from a few hundred PHP for juniors to 1,000 to 2,500 PHP and up for people who can actually ship. Hourly only helps if you can brief tightly and accept that discovery will eat the first block of time. A fixed project fee with a written scope is usually kinder to both sides.

Use a freelancer when the requirements are defined, you can answer questions the same week, you can live with one point of failure, and you do not need strategy, copy, photography, and SEO as part of the same invoice. Do not use a freelancer as a silent substitute for a team when you cannot write a brief, cannot supply content, and need the site to carry paid traffic next month.

## Agency Market Bands

Small agencies and hybrid shops often quote 50,000 to 150,000 PHP for a professional business site: more than one template, a designer and a developer, some QA, and a CMS a staffer can edit. This is the first band where you should expect a real process: sitemap, wireframes or at least page outlines, staging, and a launch list.

Mid-market agencies commonly sit around 80,000 to 300,000 PHP for customised WordPress or similar, multiple templates, component discipline, and actual review. You are paying for coordination. That only has value if it produces fewer surprises, not more meetings. Ask who designs, who builds, who writes, and who tests. If the answer is one junior with a project manager overlay, you are in freelancer economics with agency overhead.

Ecommerce on WooCommerce or Shopify in the Philippine market often lands between 70,000 and 400,000 PHP for a real catalogue, payments, shipping rules, and product templates that can rank. Cheap “online store” packages that install a plugin and leave you with 12 products in a default theme are a different product. Custom applications, portals, and booking engines commonly start around 250,000 PHP and climb into the high hundreds of thousands or more once you have user accounts, integrations, and compliance.

Metro Manila premium shops can sit above provincial averages for the same labelled project. That is not automatically quality. It can be rent, seniority, and the cost of saying no to bad scope. Provincial and remote teams can be excellent. Distance is not a discount if communication is slow.

Agency retainers for maintenance, hosting, and small edits often sit as a monthly line after launch. Budget for it. A site with no owner decays: plugins rot, forms break, SSL lapses, and the blog becomes a malware host. The project fee is not the lifetime cost.

## What Usually Sits Outside The Headline Number

Hosting, domain, email, premium theme or plugin licences, stock or custom photography, copywriting, multilingual, and payment gateway fees are commonly extra. So are content uploads if you promised to supply copy and then did not.

Integrations move the number more than gradients. CRM, booking, inventory, chat, and accounting connections need spec, credentials, and testing. If the brief says “just connect it”, the quote is a guess.

Performance work is extra when the design assumes autoplay video, font-of-the-week, and three chat widgets. It is cheaper to design with a budget for requests than to buy a speed plugin afterwards.

Migrations are extra when an existing domain has URLs that already earn clicks. Redirect maps, canonical rules, and Search Console checks are not decoration. Skipping them is how a “new website” becomes a traffic incident.

Training is extra if your staff will edit the CMS. An hour of screen-share is not a handover. A written guide plus a recorded pass through the templates you actually use will save a year of “can you change this one line” invoices.

## Freelancer Versus Agency Is A Risk Decision

Price is the obvious difference. Risk is the real one. A freelancer is a single schedule. An agency should have a bench. Should is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Ask for names. Ask what happens if the developer leaves. Ask who owns the repo, the host, the domain, and the theme licence.

Quality variance is higher in the freelance market because there is no internal review unless you pay for it. Some of the best Philippine WordPress people are independent. Some of the worst websites we crawl were also independent, shipped in a week, with five SEO plugins and a coming-soon flag still on.

Agencies fail in a different way: process without craft. Beautiful decks, slow delivery, and a CMS nobody on your team can use. Or a rebuild that ignores the old URLs. The checks in [what to look at before hiring a web design company in the Philippines](/blog/what-to-check-before-hiring-a-web-design-company-in-the-philippines/) exist because the failure mode is not only ugly pages. It is lost search demand and a site you cannot edit.

You can mix the models. A consultant or an SEO specialist can review a freelancer’s staging site before launch. An agency can bring a specialist developer for a shop that is otherwise a brochure. Split ownership without a single information architecture and a single launch list is how nothing is anyone’s fault.

## How Scope Should Drive The Band You Pick

A holding page and a WhatsApp link can live in the lowest freelance band. Do not over-buy.

A local service business that needs Google to trust it needs more: clear services, area served, proof, a working form and click-to-call, legal pages, and a Google Business Profile that matches the NAP on the site. That is usually a professional business band, freelancer or small agency, not a 10,000 PHP theme with hidden text.

A multi-location or multi-service firm needs templates that can grow without cloning. Custom post types, internal linking, and a CMS a non-developer can update belong in the mid-market agency band or with a senior freelancer who has done that IA before.

A shop needs product data quality more than it needs a parallax hero. If the catalogue is messy in the spreadsheet, it will be messy on the site. Pay for data and templates before you pay for animation.

A custom app is not a website quote with extra zeros. It is product work: users, permissions, edge cases, security. If three vendors quoted a “website” and one quoted an application, you do not have comparable bids.

## How To Compare Quotes Without Shopping On Peso Alone

Write the commercial job. Who must enquire or buy. Which devices they use. Which languages. Which URLs already earn clicks. Who will edit the site. What must work on day one versus phase two.

Ask every vendor for the same artefacts. Sitemap. Template list. CMS and who owns the licences. Staging URL. Redirect plan if you have an old site. Form and payment testing. Analytics and Search Console ownership in your accounts. A launch checklist. Warranty window for bugs.

Ask what they will not do. Vendors who promise SEO rankings, unlimited revisions, and a custom app inside a brochure fee are telling you the scope is fiction.

Give them the content you actually have, not the content you hope to write. Quotes change when the copy does not exist. Honest vendors will raise the fee or shrink the page set. Dishonest ones will ship empty headings.

Do not pay the balance before you have production access in your name. Domain, host, CMS admin, and repo should not live only in the vendor’s login. That is not distrust. It is basic continuity.

A cheaper quote that omits redirects, mobile form testing, and content is not cheaper. You will buy those later, often after rankings drop. A dearer quote that names those items is easier to audit.

## After Launch, The Meter Is Still Running

Budget updates. PHP, CMS, plugins, and certificates need care. Content will change when you add a service or a branch. Legal pages will need a pass. If you cannot do that in house, a small monthly maintenance band is part of website cost, not an upsell.

Measure the site like a commercial asset. Enquiries, call clicks, and the pages involved. If the new site is prettier and the forms fail on Android, you spent money to lose jobs. Fix that before you increase ads.

When the current site is the constraint on marketing, putting budget into templates, copy, and conversion can beat another year of channel retainers on a weak URL. When the site is already sound, do not rebuild for aesthetics and reset your search baseline for a colour change.
