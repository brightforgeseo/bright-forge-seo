On-page SEO is the work on the URL itself: what the title promises, what the heading confirms, what the first screen proves, how the page links, and whether a person who arrived from search can enquire without hunting. It is not a density score. It is not stuffing the city name into every sentence. In the Philippines it is usually the difference between a brochure that mentions a service and a page that can win the query for that service.

We treat on-page as a commercial edit. If the page cannot say what you sell, where you sell it, and what happens next, no plugin will save it. If the page says those things clearly, technical crawl and content depth have something to work with.

## One Job Per URL

Most Philippine SME sites ask the homepage to rank for every service, every city, and the brand story. That is how homepages become long and still vague. Give each paying service a URL. If you have two packages that buyers already distinguish on the phone, they probably need two pages, or one page that explains the difference without pretending they are the same keyword.

The test is simple. Could a stranger read the title and the first screen and know what to do? If they could equally be on a competitor’s site after you swap the logo, the page is not finished.

Do not clone a page and swap the city. Doorway copies fail as on-page work because they have no unique job. A location URL is justified when the office, the team, the photos, or the service area is real. A service URL is justified when you actually deliver that job.

Cannibalisation is an on-page problem. Two services, a blog post, and a campaign landing page that all target the same phrase will split clicks and confuse internal links. Pick a primary URL. Point the others at it or differentiate the intent: one page for hire-me, one for how-it-works, not two hire-me pages with different adjectives.

## Titles And Headings That Match The Search

The title tag is the result you ask Google to show. It should name the service and, when place is part of the query, the place. “Waterproofing contractor in Parañaque” beats “Number One Innovative Waterproofing Solutions Hub”. Length matters only because truncation hides the useful words. Stuffing every synonym hides them too.

The H1 should agree with the title. A title about aircon repair and an H1 about the company’s vision is a mixed signal to the engine and to the visitor. Subheadings should answer the questions that follow: what is included, how long it takes, which areas you cover, what it costs if you can say, what happens after the enquiry.

Write headings in the language the buyer uses. If sales hears “bust pipes” and the page says “comprehensive plumbing solutions”, the page is written for the agency, not for the search. Filipino and English mix in queries. Mirror the mix only when the page can stay accurate. Auto-translated headings that do not match the body are worse than English headings a bilingual buyer can still use.

Meta descriptions do not rank pages. They earn the click when the title already matched. Write a sentence that names the offer and the next step. Do not repeat the title. Do not promise a rank.

## First Screen And Proof

On a phone, the first screen is the page. The offer, the area, and the action should be visible without a slider that takes three seconds to load. Hero carousels that rotate marketing slogans are how on-page work dies in the template.

Proof belongs next to the claim. Licences, named staff, photos of the premises or the work, process steps, warranties you will honour, and project notes you are allowed to publish. “Award-winning” with no award is not on-page SEO. It is a trust leak. Stock photos of a foreign office do not help a Quezon City shop.

Experience should be first-hand. The constraints of the job, the mistakes you will not repeat, the sites you will not travel to, the difference between two similar services. That is the content a cautious buyer needs, and it is the content a generic generator will not invent accurately.

Reviews can sit on the service URL when they are about that service. A homepage wall of stars that never appears on the waterproofing page does not help the waterproofing query. Fake reviews are not an on-page tactic. They are a policy risk.

## Internal Links And Information Architecture

On-page includes the links on the page and the links to the page. The header should list the commercial services, not hide them behind “what we do” with no names. The homepage should send people to those URLs with descriptive anchors. Related services can link to each other when a buyer truly compares them.

Blog posts should point at the service they were actually about. A paragraph about permits that never links to the permitting service is a wasted page. Footer links that repeat fifty cities are a leftover from doorway thinking. A short footer with real services and contact is enough.

Breadcrumbs help users and crawlers when they match the real hierarchy. They should not invent a category the site does not have.

Orphan pages cannot rank well because nothing internal says they matter. After you publish a service URL, link it from the homepage, the menu, and any article that already attracts related impressions. That is on-page work. It is also the cheapest authority you control.

## Content Depth Without A Novel

Depth means the page answers the buying questions, not that it hit a word count. What the job includes. What it excludes. Typical timeline. Areas covered. What the customer must prepare. How quoting works. What happens after they submit.

[Content SEO in the Philippines](/content-seo-services-philippines/) is the layer that creates missing URLs and deeper supporting pages. On-page is making the URL you already have do its job. We keep them separate so a thin service template does not get a twelve-month blog calendar instead of a rewrite.

Do not paste a keyword list into the body. Do not repeat the city in every H2. Do not write a history of SEO on a plumbing page. Do not publish FAQ accordion filler that repeats the same sentence with a question mark.

FAQs are useful when they are the real objections: “Do you serve Cainta on Saturdays?” “Do we need to be home?” “Do you pull permits?” Mark them up as FAQ only if they are visible. Invisible schema FAQs are a mismatch.

Media needs alt text that describes the image, file names that are not IMG_4032, and captions only when they add a fact. Alt text is not a place to dump queries.

## Conversion Is Part Of On-Page

A ranking page that cannot collect the enquiry is a leak. One primary action. A visible phone number that works on tap. A form with only the fields you use. If the market lives in Viber, Messenger, or WhatsApp, say so and make the path obvious. Do not pretend a form is monitored when it is not.

Trust copy next to the form matters in the Philippines because fraud anxiety is real. Who will call. Whether a site visit is billed. Whether photos can start a quote. That text is on-page SEO because it keeps the visitor on the URL long enough to act.

Popups that cover the first screen, chat widgets that block the call button, and sticky bars that hide the H1 on mobile are on-page failures even when the title tag is perfect.

Measure the action. If you cannot tell a qualified organic enquiry from a job application, you cannot tell whether the on-page edit worked. Call tracking or a dedicated number is a page decision, not a later analytics project.

## Philippine Patterns That Break On-Page Work

Page builders often have three title fields. The theme H1, the SEO plugin title, and the social title. They drift. We align them on the commercial templates, then lock a pattern so new pages do not invent a fourth.

Duplicate product descriptions from suppliers make every SKU look the same. On-page work is unique specs, unique photos, and unique shipping or warranty notes, not a synonym pass.

Bilingual sites split the job. If /en/ and the default language are copies, pick a primary for each query class or invest in real translations. Partial translation, where the nav is English and the body is Filipino with leftover English widgets, reads as unfinished to users and to crawlers.

Local modifiers get abused. Adding “Manila Cebu Davao nationwide” to every title is not coverage. It is a title that ranks for nothing in particular. Geography belongs where it is true: a location page, a service-area paragraph, a profile.

Legal and medical sites cannot use the same proof as a cafe. On-page still applies. It just cannot invent outcomes, testimonials you cannot source, or offices you do not have.

## How We Edit A Page

We read Search Console for that URL: queries, impressions, clicks, and the average position as a diagnostic. We read the live page on a phone. We note the mismatch: query says X, H1 says Y, proof is missing, form is below unrelated content, internal links point elsewhere.

Then we change the title, H1, first screen, subheads, proof, links, and action. We do not “optimise” by adding a keyword to the footer. We ship the edit and watch the same query set, not the whole domain.

If the URL has no impressions, the problem may be discovery, not on-page. If it has impressions and no clicks, the title and description are the first suspects. If it has clicks and no enquiries, the page body and the form are the suspects. That triage stops us from rewriting a page that is actually blocked by noindex.

## What To Leave Off The Page

Leave off ranking guarantees, unverifiable “best in the Philippines” lines, and competitor smears. Leave off keyword lists. Leave off city paragraphs for places you do not serve. Leave off stock statistics with no source. Leave off author names that are not real people.

Leave off a second H1. Leave off hidden text. Leave off identical meta on every URL. Leave off “click here” as the only internal anchor.

Those omissions are on-page quality. They are also how you avoid a cleanup when a later team inherits the site.
