# US Ecommerce Brands Using a Philippines SEO Team

US ecommerce brands usually hire a Philippines SEO team for the same reason they hire overseas developers: catalog work does not sleep, and the site has more URLs than an in-house marketer can inspect. That hire fails when the brief is "write blogs and build links" while the real ranking surface is collection templates, product variants, faceted search, and a crawl budget that Google already spends on filtered junk.

We run SEO for US storefronts from the Philippines. The work is not a cheaper copy of a US agency blog calendar. It is template-level diagnosis, merchandising language that matches how Americans search, and a production loop that your developers and ecommerce manager can actually ship. If your catalog is the business, treat the SEO team as catalog infrastructure, not as a content vendor parked next to it.

## The Ranking Surface Is The Catalog, Not The Journal

Most US Shopify, BigCommerce, Magento, and custom stacks lose organic revenue on templates. Collection pages that share one thin paragraph. Product titles that are SKU-first. Variant URLs that index as duplicates. Faceted parameters that create thousands of near-copies. Out-of-stock products that 404 or stay live with no alternative. Pagination that never consolidates. Those defects do not get fixed by a lifestyle blog.

A Philippines team can work those problems if you give them the merchandising truth. Which collections are money. Which products are seasonal. Which variants are the same item in a different color. Which filters are useful to shoppers and which exist because the theme came with them. Without that, we will guess from the sitemap, and the sitemap is often the problem.

Write the commercial map before the content map. Name the top 20 collections by revenue and by search demand. Name the product types that must rank on google.com, not on a global mix of country stores. If you have a .com that ships only in the US, say so. If you have regional domains or Shopify Markets, say which storefront owns US search. Mixed hreflang and mixed currency on the same URL set will waste months.

Our [ecommerce SEO services](/ecommerce-seo-services/) start there: the templates Google actually crawls, the internal links collections use, and the product copy that either helps a shopper choose or repeats the manufacturer PDF.

## Give The Team Crawl Reality, Not A Keyword Spreadsheet

US catalogs generate crawl demand that a 40-page brochure site never sees. Googlebot will sample parameter URLs, tagged pages, search-result pages inside the site, and old promo collections you forgot to noindex. A Philippines SEO team that cannot see server logs, Search Console crawl stats, and the live robots.txt is working blind.

Share Search Console at property level, including the URL inspection and page indexing reports. Share how you currently block parameters: robots.txt, canonicals, meta robots, or Shopify's default. Those methods are not interchangeable. A robots.txt disallow stops Google from crawling, which also stops it from seeing a noindex on the page. A canonical that nobody respects because every facet is linked in the main nav will not save you.

Ask the team to produce a crawl budget note in the first month. Which URL patterns should be crawled. Which should be left alone. Which internal links currently waste Googlebot on sort orders. That note should name paths, not vibes. "Clean up parameters" is not a ticket. "Stop linking color= and sort= from collection templates, and keep those URLs out of the XML sitemap" is a ticket.

If your engineers own robots and headers, put their names in the working group. Offshore SEO without a US-hours or overlap deploy path means tickets sit. Manila can write the spec the same day. Someone still has to merge it.

## Merchandising Language Is A Ranking Input

US shoppers search in US English, with US product names, sizes, and seasonal language. A Philippines writer who is fluent in English can still miss sneakers versus trainers, fall sale versus autumn, and the way Americans write unit sizes. The fix is not a style lecture. It is a merchandising glossary and examples of titles you would put on the live product.

Give us the words your ads already convert on. Paid search is often a better glossary than an SEO tool for ecommerce, because it is tied to revenue. Give us the words customer support hears. Give us the manufacturer names you must keep exact, and the marketing adjectives you want banned from H1s.

Collection copy should describe the range and the buying constraints, not a brand essay. Product copy should answer fit, compatibility, materials, shipping reality, and the difference between variants. Schema should match the visible offer: price, availability, and reviews you actually have. Invented review stars and mismatched prices are not an SEO win. They are a trust problem that Google and shoppers both notice.

If your catalog is too large for unique copy on every SKU, say so in the brief. We will then scope unique copy for money SKUs and tighter templates for the long tail, instead of pretending 40,000 unique essays will ship. Honesty about volume is how a Philippines team stays useful.

## Internal Links And Facets Decide What Google Thinks You Sell

Ecommerce SEO is mostly information architecture. Collections that do not link to sibling collections. Products orphaned from the category they belong to. Blogs that never point at the SKU they describe. Related products driven only by a randomizer. Facets in the crawl path that look like extra categories.

A Philippines SEO team should be allowed to propose IA changes, not only meta titles. That includes which filters deserve indexable landing pages (size 12 running shoes in a real demand cluster) and which must stay noindex (sort by price). It includes breadcrumb rules. It includes whether a "shop all" collection should exist at all.

US brands often want a blog because a competitor has one. Sometimes the competitor ranks because the catalog is cleaner, and the blog is decoration. We will still use content when it supports a buying question the template cannot answer: compatibility charts, care, sizing, and comparisons you can stand behind. We will not use content as a substitute for fixing the collection that should rank.

## Operations That Make An Offshore Team Work On A Live Store

Ecommerce sites change daily. Prices, stock, new colors, flash collections, app installs that inject new URL patterns. The SEO team needs a merchandising calendar and a rule for apps.

Every new Shopify app that creates pages is a crawl event. Reviews apps, translation apps, wishlist apps, and "SEO optimizer" apps have all created duplicate titles, extra parameters, or sitewide noindex mistakes. The brief should say that no SEO or redirect app goes live without a crawl check. Your Philippines team should be on that check, not told after the app is already indexing.

Stock rules need a written policy. When a product is gone for good, redirect to the closest in-stock parent or collection, and do not leave a 404 that still sits in internal links. When a product is temporarily out, keep the URL, say so, and point to alternatives. US shoppers and Google both hate dead ends. A team in Manila can maintain that map if they can see inventory status or at least a weekly merchandising export.

Reporting should follow revenue pages. Rankings for a blog about "holiday gift ideas" are not a substitute for collection landing-page clicks and product-page conversion from organic. We report from GA4 and Search Console against the money collections you named, plus crawl anomalies. If you want vanity keyword trackers, say so, but do not let them run the month.

## What US Brands Should Put In The Retainer

A usable US ecommerce retainer with a Philippines team has four lanes with owners.

Lane one: technical and indexation. Crawl, robots, canonicals, sitemaps, pagination, duplicate variants, speed on mobile collection templates. Lane two: on-page templates. Title patterns, H1 rules, internal links, schema that matches the live product. Lane three: merchandising content. Money collections and SKUs, plus supporting guides only where the template cannot do the job. Lane four: digital PR and links that point at those money URLs, not at a blog nobody uses.

Each lane needs an acceptance test. "Titles updated" is not a test. "Money collection titles unique, under the SERP pixel limit we agreed, and live on the template" is a test. "Links acquired" is not a test. "Referring domain relevant to the product category, pointing at a live collection or product, no paid directory dump" is a test.

US hours overlap is a planning problem, not a slogan. Put decisions that need your merchandising lead in a twice-weekly window. Let Manila ship copy and tickets outside that window. Do not require a US 9am standup that burns the overlap on status. Status belongs in the ticket system.

## Red Flags Specific To Ecommerce Outsourcing

Be wary of a team that wants to start with 12 blogs before they have opened a collection template. Be wary of anyone who treats Shopify's theme editor as the whole technical surface. Be wary of automatic synonym spinning on product titles. Be wary of "add 500 products to the sitemap" as a growth plan. Be wary of international SEO advice that copies hreflang from a blog post and never inspects your Markets setup.

Also be wary of the opposite: a technical team that never reads a product. Crawl cleanliness with dead merchandising copy still loses to a competitor who explains the kit. The Philippines advantage is that you can fund both lanes in one retainer if you scope them. US domestic retainers often fund only one because the hourly rate eats the other.

If you already have a US agency for brand and want offshore execution, write the RACI. Two teams rewriting titles is worse than one slow team.
