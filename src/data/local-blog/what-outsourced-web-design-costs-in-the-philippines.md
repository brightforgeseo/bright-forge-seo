A peso figure without a workload is not a web design price. It is a guess. Buyers still ask what it costs to outsource web design in the Philippines because finance needs a band before anyone will open a proposal. That is fair. The mistake is treating a ₱25,000 brochure theme and a custom lead-generation build as the same product because both are “a website”.

The figures below are market commentary from public Philippine and outsourcing write-ups in 2026. They are not Bright Forge prices, and they are not a quote. Local SME guides commonly put basic brochure sites around ₱15,000 to ₱45,000, professional business sites around ₱50,000 to ₱120,000, and ecommerce or heavier custom work from roughly ₱70,000 into the mid ₱200,000s and beyond. Custom applications are often quoted from about ₱300,000. Freelancer project ranges frequently sit below agency ranges. International-facing teams quote in dollars, with mid-level development labour often discussed in the $20 to $35 an hour band and senior labour higher. Treat every number as a cluster, not a rate card.

## What The Market Bands Usually Buy

The ₱15,000 to ₱45,000 band is almost always a template, a short page list, a contact form, and a freelancer or a very small shop. You might get a homepage, about, services, contact, and a blog stub on WordPress or a site builder. You will rarely get original information architecture, custom templates for every service, or a developer who will argue about canonicals and Core Web Vitals. That band can be enough for a cafe, a single-location trade, or a personal brand if you already have copy and photos. It is not enough for a multi-service B2B site that needs to rank and convert.

The ₱50,000 to ₱120,000 band is where many “professional business site” quotes live. Expect a designed theme or a light customisation, 10 to 30 pages, forms, analytics, and basic on-page setup. Some of this work is real. The constraint is depth. If you need unique layouts per service, a resource library, multilingual templates, or a CMS your staff can actually edit without breaking the header, you are already pressing the top of the band or leaving it.

Agency-shaped custom work commonly appears from about ₱80,000 to ₱250,000 for sites with original layouts, more than a brochure sitemap, performance work, and a designer plus developer plus someone who manages the project. Ecommerce catalogues in public 2026 guides often sit from about ₱70,000 to ₱250,000 and up, depending on product count, payments, and theme versus custom product templates. Custom web apps, booking platforms, and membership systems sit in a different conversation, often ₱300,000 and up, because you are no longer buying pages. You are buying software.

Dollar quotes for overseas buyers follow the same logic with a different currency. Public outsourcing notes still describe simple Philippine business sites in a roughly $1,000 to $2,700 cluster, and custom applications in a much wider $9,000 to $50,000-plus spread. Hourly developer bands published for 2026 often show mid-level freelance or contract labour around $22 to $58 an hour, with agency blended rates higher because you are paying for project management and QA, not only typing. Those spreads exist because “web design” is not one SKU.

If a quote sits far below the band that matches the sitemap, the missing hours come from somewhere. They usually come from a theme you cannot leave, stock photos, copy you must write yourself, no staging, no QA on mobile, or a handoff with no documentation.

## What Moves The Number Besides Taste

Page count is the obvious lever and a poor one on its own. Twenty thin pages cost less than eight pages with real templates, proof blocks, and forms that post to the right inbox. Ask the quote to name templates, not only pages. A service template reused well is cheaper and better than twenty one-off layouts that nobody can maintain.

CMS choice changes cost twice: at build, and every year after. WordPress remains the default in the Philippine market because clients can hire locally to edit it. Headless, custom Laravel, or a proprietary agency CMS can be the right engineering choice. They are the wrong commercial choice if the only people who can change a title work at the vendor. Hosting, licences, and plugin renewals belong in the same conversation. A cheap build that needs ₱8,000 a month of plugins is not cheap.

Content and assets are where quotes go to die. If you do not have service copy, staff photos, licences, and product data, the designer will invent lorem, or the project will stall while someone writes. Writing is not free because it was left out of the web design line. Photography is not free because a theme has a slider.

Integrations add hours in chunks: booking, payments, CRM, live chat, inventory, membership, and multilingual routing. Each one has a happy path and a failure path. A quote that lists “WooCommerce” without product count, tax rules, and shipping is not a quote yet.

Implementation access changes cost more than buyers expect. If the team can publish, hours go into the site. If every change needs a staging ticket and a two-week release, the same scope costs more. That is queue time, not padding.

SEO-ready structure is not a plugin toggle. Information architecture, title logic, heading hierarchy, internal links, schema that matches visible content, and an enquiry path are design decisions. They are cheaper to put in at build than to retrofit after a pretty theme ships with duplicate titles and a contact form that goes nowhere. ## Freelancer, Agency, And Dedicated Hire Are Different Products

A freelancer in the ₱15,000 to ₱60,000 project band, or a dollar hourly rate at the lower end of public 2026 ranges, can be the right buy when the sitemap is short, you can manage the project, and you accept a single point of failure. The risk is availability, undocumented decisions, and a site nobody else can extend.

An agency quote in the ₱80,000 to ₱300,000-plus cluster usually includes design, development, and someone who will schedule you. You pay for coordination and a wider toolkit. You should also get a written scope, staging, and a handoff. If you do not, you paid agency money for freelancer process.

A dedicated designer or developer through a staffing partner is a third product. Public 2026 notes for Philippine design and development seats often sit in the low thousands of dollars a month fully loaded, far below a US hire, and far above a one-off ₱20,000 brochure. That model fits ongoing product work. It is a poor fit if you need one brochure site this quarter and then silence.

[Outsourcing web design versus hiring in-house](/blog/outsource-web-design-vs-hiring-in-house/) is the same decision with a longer time horizon. A project fee buys a launch. A salary buys a person who still needs briefs, QA, and someone who owns the commercial outcome. Mixing them without a rule is how companies pay twice: once for a build that cannot be edited, then again for a hire who spends three months reverse-engineering it.

## How To Read A Quote Without Shopping On The Floor Price

Ask what is in the sitemap, which templates are unique, which CMS, who writes copy, who supplies photos, what browsers and devices are tested, and what “SEO included” actually means. If SEO included is Yoast installed and a favicon, it is not SEO. If it is titles, headings, indexation, and an enquiry path that matches the offer, it is part of the build.

Ask for staging, training, and a list of logins you will own. Domain, hosting, analytics, Search Console, and the CMS should be in the business’s name. Proprietary platforms that only the vendor can edit turn the build cost into a rent.

Ask what happens after launch. A year of “free support” that is actually five small text edits is not a retainer. A maintenance band should name hours, plugin updates, backups, and what is out of scope. Ongoing design changes are a different fee from keeping the lights on.

Ask them to walk through one competitor URL you admire and say what they would not copy. Taste without judgement produces clones. Judgement without taste produces grey boxes. You need both, and you can hear both in ten minutes if you ask about a live site instead of a moodboard.

Red flags travel with cheap and expensive quotes alike. Guaranteed first-page rankings bundled into a design fee. Unlimited pages for a fixed peso figure. Stock screenshots of dashboards as if they were your analytics. Contracts that keep the repo. Scope that says “responsive, SEO, social media, branding, content” in one line. That line is how a ₱40,000 project becomes a fight.

## Budget For The Year, Not Only The Invoice

The build is the visible cost. The real cost includes copy, photography, licences, hosting, a cookie and privacy baseline if you collect data, and the hours your staff will spend feeding the CMS. It includes the cost of being wrong: a theme you cannot leave, a page types that cannot rank, a form that drops enquiries on mobile.

If you already know the site must generate leads, put measurement in the first statement of work. Analytics, Search Console, call tracking if you use it, and a thank-you page are cheap on day one and awkward in month four. If you already know you will run SEO after launch, do not buy a builder that fights titles, headings, and crawlable HTML. Retrofit is where outsourced design earns its bad reputation.

Currency and tax matter for overseas buyers. A quote in PHP and a quote in USD are not comparable until you know what is included, who pays VAT, and whether revisions are capped. Time zone overlap is part of cost: cheap hours that only exist while you sleep will burn senior time on your side.

Do not split the difference by hiring the cheapest vendor “to see how it goes” if the work is hard to reverse. A bad theme, a locked page builder, and duplicated content are expensive to unwind. Cheap tests are only cheap when you can throw the artefact away.

Owners sometimes compare a ₱20,000 site that produces no enquiries with a ₱120,000 site that does, and still call the first one better value. Value is qualified enquiries and a site you can change, not the lowest line in a spreadsheet.
